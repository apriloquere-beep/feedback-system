# SFMS v3 5,000-sample multilingual dataset quality audit

## Scope and method

- Dataset reviewed: `data/labeled/sfms_sentiment_v3_5000.csv`
- Random selection: 100 records per language, sampled without replacement with Python `random.Random(20261117)`.
- Total manually inspected: 300 records.
- Sample label counts:

| Language | Positive | Negative | Neutral | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| English | 31 | 35 | 34 | 100 |
| Filipino/Tagalog | 37 | 31 | 32 | 100 |
| Bisaya/Cebuano | 29 | 35 | 36 | 100 |

This is a qualitative sample review, not a native-speaker certification or a full audit of all 5,000 rows. Filipino/Tagalog and Cebuano should be checked by fluent reviewers familiar with local healthcare terminology.

## Findings

### Overall

- The dataset is visibly template-generated. Many records repeat the same sentence frames, actions, and visit contexts.
- Records commonly join two unrelated service experiences into one feedback entry. This weakens coherence and makes the text unlike a single, naturally written comment.
- Generic frames are composed with details that do not fit their grammar or meaning. Some neutral frames recursively wrap another instruction, producing nonsensical requests.
- No clear positive-versus-negative polarity inversion was evident in this sample: positive and negative rows generally contain directionally consistent wording, while neutral rows are usually requests or suggestions. This does not verify each label; the labels are strongly encoded by the templates, and topic/meaning fit still needs human review.
- These independently generated language rows are not verified translation pairs, so translation equivalence was not assessed.

### English

- Nested neutral instructions and awkward verb-object combinations occur.
- Some topic headings have subject-verb disagreement, and some detail phrases duplicate wording already present in the action template.
- Two separate topics are often combined in a single record.

Examples:

| Record ID | Issue |
|---|---|
| `SFMSV3-EN-U-0328` | “Could a short notice explain how to post a short guide explaining…” recursively nests instructions. |
| `SFMSV3-EN-U-0303` | “Could a short notice explain how to post a map showing how to reach…” has the same nested-frame problem. |
| `SFMSV3-EN-P-0166` | “checked the requirements for requirements for the laboratory test” duplicates “requirements for.” |
| `SFMSV3-EN-N-0123` | “The medical documents and certificates was frustrating” has agreement/heading problems; it also combines unrelated topics. |
| `SFMSV3-EN-N-0304` | “during clinic hours during a visit after a referral” repeats the temporal construction. |

### Filipino/Tagalog

- Some actor/action combinations mix active and passive constructions, making the actor appear to perform an incorrectly formed verb phrase.
- Repeated nouns and prepositions create cumbersome wording; some directional phrases are duplicated.
- Repeated context clauses and unrelated service topics make entries sound mechanically assembled.

Examples:

| Record ID | Issue |
|---|---|
| `SFMSV3-TL-P-0448` | “ang kawani ng talaan ay ipinaliwanag ang mga hakbang” has an awkward actor/verb construction; the sentence needs a consistent active or passive form. |
| `SFMSV3-TL-N-0275` | “ang kawani ng talaan ay naantala ang serbisyong…” confuses the staff member as subject with the delayed service as object. |
| `SFMSV3-TL-U-0288` | “listahan ng mga kailangan para sa mga kailangan para sa pagsusuri” repeats the requirements phrase. |
| `SFMSV3-TL-P-0133` | “daan papunta sa daan papunta sa klinika” duplicates the directional phrase. |
| `SFMSV3-TL-P-0240` | “ipinaliwanag ang mga hakbang para sa kung kailan handa ang resulta” combines incompatible constructions. |

### Bisaya/Cebuano

- Several records have agent/verb-order or active/passive problems. These should be corrected by a fluent Cebuano reviewer rather than by literal English-to-Cebuano substitutions.
- Some expressions read like literal template translations, especially phrases for leaving someone unsure or delaying a service.
- Repeated or near-synonymous wording and unrelated topic pairing are common.

Examples:

| Record ID | Issue |
|---|---|
| `SFMSV3-CEB-P-0421` | “ang kawani sa mga rekord giandam ang impormasyon…” has an awkward agent/verb construction. |
| `SFMSV3-CEB-N-0146` | “ang grupo sa pag-atiman gitapos ang panag-istorya…” mixes the actor with a passive-form verb. |
| `SFMSV3-CEB-N-0346` | “ang tigtambag nagbilin kanako nga wala masayod…” sounds like a literal construction for leaving someone unsure; review for an idiomatic Cebuano expression. |
| `SFMSV3-CEB-U-0432` | “balita bahin sa pagkalangan” is repetitive (“news/update about a delay”). |
| `SFMSV3-CEB-U-0113` | “lista sa mga kinahanglanon alang sa asa isumite ang porma” does not fit the list-of-requirements frame. |

## Recommended corrections (not applied)

1. Make each feedback record express one coherent service experience or request; avoid pairing unrelated topic families into a single text.
2. Replace recursive neutral templates with direct, single-layer requests.
3. Use category- and language-specific sentence patterns whose verbs accept the detail type; avoid inserting phrases such as “requirements for…” after a template that already supplies those words.
4. Have fluent Filipino/Tagalog and Cebuano reviewers rewrite—not merely approve—unnatural constructions, actor/verb forms, vocabulary, and regional usage.
5. Review positive, negative, and neutral labels against the whole record, including whether neutral suggestions are genuinely neutral and whether records with mixed polarity should be split.
6. After approved manual edits, rerun exact duplicate, near-duplicate, split leakage, label distribution, and provenance checks. Keep related/paraphrased records in the same split.

## Human-review checklist

For every record, reviewers should:

- [ ] Confirm the text is natural and grammatically correct in its declared language and dialect.
- [ ] Check spelling, punctuation, code-switching, and healthcare terminology.
- [ ] Confirm the record describes one coherent experience or request.
- [ ] Check that the selected topic and details match the described event.
- [ ] Verify the sentiment label against the full text; flag mixed or ambiguous sentiment.
- [ ] Compare against other records for exact, near, and meaning-level duplicates.
- [ ] If a translation relationship is claimed, compare meaning and sentiment with its source text; otherwise do not imply equivalence.
- [ ] Check for names, contact details, patient identifiers, or accidental real-case details.
- [ ] Record reviewer identity, date, language expertise, decision, and notes.
- [ ] Keep provenance marked Synthetic and review status Pending until review is completed and recorded; never relabel synthetic text as real patient feedback.

## Data integrity

No sample was generated, edited, deleted, or relabeled during this audit. The 5,000 records remain `source_group=generated_synthetic_v3` and `review_status=Pending`. The original 900-row master and production sentiment analyzer were not modified. No model was trained.
